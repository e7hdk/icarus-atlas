HER STORY

Though married to Hephaestus, she loved Ares. Helios, who sees all things, told the smith-god of the affair; Hephaestus forged a net of bronze too fine to see and too strong to break, and snared the lovers in their bed for all the gods to look upon.

— Homer · Ovid — Odyssey 8.266–366; Metamorphoses 4.169–189

In the oldest account, Eros is no child of hers: Hesiod numbers him among the very first beings of the cosmos, already ancient when Aphrodite rose from the foam. Later poets shrink that elder power into her unruly little son — bribed with a golden ball to strike Medea, loosed upon Apollo and on Dis himself, so that even the lord of the dead is drawn into her dominion.

— Theogony 116–122; Argonautica 3.83–159; Metamorphoses 1.452–473, 5.363–384
