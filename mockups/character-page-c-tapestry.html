HES
I

BORN OF THE FOAM

The immortal flesh of Uranus fell into the restless sea, and a white foam gathered around it; within it a goddess grew. She drifted past holy Cythera and rose from the waves at Cyprus, and grass sprang up beneath her slender feet. Eros and Desire attended her from the first.
— Theogony 188–206 · ⚖ Homer tells it otherwise
HOM
II

THE WOUND AT TROY

Shielding her son Aeneas on the battlefield, she was pierced at the wrist by Diomedes and fled to Olympus, to be comforted in her mother Dione’s arms — while Zeus smiled, and told her that the works of war were not for her.
— Iliad 5.330–430
H·O
III

THE NET OF BRONZE

Married to Hephaestus, she loved Ares. Helios, who sees all things, told the smith-god of the affair; Hephaestus forged a net of bronze too fine to see and too strong to break, and snared the lovers in their bed for all the gods to look upon.
— Odyssey 8.266–366; Metamorphoses 4.169–189
A·O
IV

THE UNRULY SON

Later poets shrink primordial Eros into her winged and unruly boy, the instrument of her will: bribed with a marvellous golden ball to shoot Medea with desire, loosed upon Apollo with the golden arrow, and upon Dis himself — so that even the lord of the dead is drawn into her dominion.
— Argonautica 3.83–159; Metamorphoses 1.452–473, 5.363–384 · ⚖ in Hesiod, Eros is older than her
