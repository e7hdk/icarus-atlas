## 1. Why this dynasty

1. **A second autochthony.** Beside the Argive first-man (Phoroneus) and the Attic Cecropids, Arcadia gives the third great earth-born line: Pelasgus, the acorn-eating first king, and the deluge-bringing impiety of Lycaon.
2. **It carries two famous catasterisms.** Callisto becomes the Great Bear and Arcas the Bear-warden (Boötes/Arctophylax) — the sky-myths the atlas's "galaxy" frame loves.
3. **It completes Atalanta.** The atlas's `atalanta` was deliberately entered thin (the boar-hunt guest) with her Arcadian saga deferred; this house supplies her disputed parentage, her footrace, and her son Parthenopaeus.
4. **It interlocks widely with the standing sky.** `zeus` (father of Arcas), `artemis` (Callisto's mistress), `hermes`/`atlas` (via the Pleiad Maia who reared Arcas), `meleager`/`ares` (Atalanta's circle), `jason` (the Tegean Argonauts Ancaeus, Cepheus, Amphidamas), `proetus`/`amyclas`/`tyndareus` (Tegean marriages).
5. **Severe homonyms and a live id-collision** (`clymene`, `pallas` already exist).

## 7. Relation & data-modeling notes

1. `parent` child→parent; the three disputes are competing topic-tagged edges on `atalanta` (two fathers, two husbands) and `parthenopaeus` (three fathers).
2. Callisto: `zeus` lover, `callisto`→`lycaon` parent (dominant), `arcas`→`callisto`/`zeus`; the bear-transformation and Artemis' shot narrated in prose (the transformer dispute resists clean edge-modeling).
3. Maia: `maia`→`atlas` parent (Pleiad), `hermes`→`maia` parent (existing Hermes gets a mother edge), and Maia as Arcas' nurse in prose.
4. Telephus' father Heracles is **prose only** until a Heracles node exists (record Auge→Telephus as a single mother edge, Heracles narrated).
5. No lineage/residences (Arcadia is a region, not a flagship city); the cities Tegea/Mantinea/Stymphalus/Psophis may enter the geo layer separately.
6. Boundary non-nodes (prose): Heracles, Deucalion's flood, Asclepius, Coronis, Nauplius, Teuthras, Cybele, the Stymphalian birds.

## 9. Conclusion

Arcadia is the wild heart of the Peloponnese and the atlas's third autochthony: from the acorn-king Pelasgus and the wolf Lycaon descend the Bear and her son the eponym, the three branches that fill the highland, the Tegean house that breeds Telephus and the boar-slain Ancaeus, and — at last given her parents and her footrace — the huntress Atalanta. Build the root and the catasterisms first (Batch A), the branches and Tegea second (Batch B), Atalanta's saga and the Aleids third (Batch C); resolve the `clymene`/`pallas` collisions and the homonym suffixes up front, keep Heracles a boundary frame, and preserve the parentage and footrace forks as first-class disputes.
